HELP TIMUR

A student. A university. An impossible semester.

My name is Timur. I am a student. And I need your help. Until recently, Timur was an ordinary young man with ordinary dreams and an ordinary amount of free time. He had plans for his future. He had hobbies. He had friends. He could wake up in the morning without immediately reaching for his phone to check whether a professor had uploaded something new. He could open his laptop without feeling a strange sense of fear. He could look at a calendar and see dates instead of deadlines. Those were simpler times. We did not know how quickly everything could change.

When Timur first entered university, we were proud of him. We thought university would be a place where he could learn new things, meet interesting people, and prepare himself for a successful future. We believed that studying would be difficult, but manageable. We believed that if he worked hard, attended his classes, completed his assignments and prepared for his exams, everything would be fine. We were optimistic. Perhaps too optimistic. Nobody warned us about what would happen when one assignment became two assignments, and two assignments became five. Nobody explained that an exam scheduled for next month could somehow become tomorrow. Nobody told us that "you have plenty of time" is one of the most dangerous sentences a university student can hear.

At first, Timur handled everything normally. He would come home, open his computer, look at the assignment, and tell himself that he would finish it later. This seemed reasonable. Later was a perfectly acceptable time to complete an assignment. Unfortunately, later kept moving. Later became tonight. Tonight became tomorrow. Tomorrow became the weekend. The weekend became next week. And eventually next week became the night before the deadline.

We remember the first time Timur said, "I'll do it tomorrow." Nobody thought much of it. Students say things like that all the time. But then he said it again. And again. And again. Eventually, the phrase stopped sounding like a plan and started sounding like a cry for help.

Then the exams began approaching. At first, they were far away. There was plenty of time. Timur was not worried. Then they were closer. Then they were very close. Then there was an exam tomorrow morning, and Timur suddenly discovered that the human brain is not designed to learn an entire semester overnight.

This was the beginning of the situation we are facing today. Timur started drinking more coffee. He started sleeping later. He started opening documents and then staring at them for several minutes before remembering why he had opened them. His browser became increasingly difficult to understand. There were tabs for university websites, tabs for articles, tabs for lecture notes, tabs for assignments, tabs containing videos he was supposed to watch for class, and at least three tabs that nobody could explain.

We asked him how many tabs he had open. He refused to answer.

We knew then that the situation was serious.

The problem is not simply that Timur has a lot of work. The problem is that the work never seems to end. Every time he finishes one task, another appears. Every time he submits an assignment, another deadline is waiting somewhere in the distance. Every time he thinks he can finally relax, a notification appears on his phone.

This has had a serious effect on his daily life. His sleep schedule is no longer a schedule. It is a suggestion. His weekends have become extensions of weekdays. His evenings have become study sessions. His study sessions have become periods of staring at the same paragraph for twenty minutes while thinking about absolutely anything except the paragraph.

Sometimes Timur sits down to study at eight o'clock in the evening. He tells himself he will work until ten. This is a reasonable plan. At 8:05 he opens the document. At 8:12 he checks something online. At 8:19 he checks something else. At 8:27 he decides he needs water. At 8:31 he returns to the computer. At 8:42 he remembers that he has another assignment. At 8:51 he looks at the clock. At 9:03 he decides that the evening is still young.

At 11:47 PM, he finally begins.

This is what university has done to him.

There was a time when Timur could distinguish between Monday and Friday. Now the days have merged together. There are only two categories of time: before the deadline and after the deadline.

Sometimes we ask him if he is okay.

He says yes.

We know he is not.

So please help him do exactly that.

Share this page. Wish him luck. Send encouragement. And above all, remind him that the semester must go on.

Thank you for your support.
